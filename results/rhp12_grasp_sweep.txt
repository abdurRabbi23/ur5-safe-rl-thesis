 READ q_final FIRST — it says WHICH failure you have:
   q_final ~ 1.00  the fingers closed all the way with nothing in the way, so
                   they never touched the cube. That is a GEOMETRY or COLLIDER
                   problem (wrong offset band, or the convex decomposition
                   produced no usable pad geometry). Friction is irrelevant.
   q_final ~ 0.78  the fingers stalled on the cube. Contact is real and the
                   residual position error is being converted into clamp force.
                   If it still dropped, NOW it is a friction / effort problem.

 GRIP HOLDS at 3/3 offsets: ['0.125', '0.130', '0.135']

   offset 0.130 m has face_gap 0.0413 vs cube 0.0412  (delta +0.0001)
   -> that is the TRUE PAD GRIP. Prefer it for TCP_OFFSET.

 Sanity checks before you lock it in:
   * face_gap MUCH WIDER than the cube but still 'HELD' means the cube is
     wedged on the curved r1/l1 links, not held by the flat pads. It will
     hold in a static test and fail under the accelerations of a real lift.
   * face_gap NARROWER than the cube means the pads are interpenetrating it;
     the hold is partly solver depenetration, not friction.
   * if EVERY swept offset held, the band is truncated — extend the sweep
     until you find the edge, then re-read this line.
